Clinical Brain Tumor Pathology Reference

Detailed radiological characteristics, anatomical localization, and diagnostic criteria for each class in the dataset.

Malignant / Infiltrative

Glioma

Tumors originating in glial supporting cells (astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, ependymal cells). Encompasses low-grade astrocytomas to high-grade glioblastomas (GBM).

Anatomical Origin: Cerebral hemispheres, brainstem, spinal cord.
MRI Appearance: Heterogeneous signal, irregular rim enhancement, surrounding vasogenic edema, mass effect.
Severity: High / Critical
Typically Benign / Extra-Axial

Meningioma

Slow-growing tumors arising from arachnoid cap cells of the meninges surrounding the brain and spinal cord.

Anatomical Origin: Dural coverings, parasagittal convexity, sphenoid ridge.
MRI Appearance: Well-demarcated extra-axial mass, homogeneous vivid post-contrast enhancement, "dural tail" sign.
Severity: Moderate
Sellar / Endocrine

Pituitary Adenoma

Neoplasms arising from anterior pituitary glandular cells in the sella turcica, often causing endocrine or visual disturbance.

Anatomical Origin: Sella turcica at base of skull, adjacent to optic chiasm.
MRI Appearance: Sellar remodeling, suprasellar extension with classic "figure-of-eight" or "snowman" appearance.
Severity: Moderate
Healthy / Normal Scan

No Tumor (Normal)

Healthy cranial scan showing normal brain parenchyma, preserved ventricles, and absence of mass lesions or midline shift.

Anatomical Status: Preserved sulcal-gyral pattern, midline symmetry.
MRI Appearance: Symmetric ventricles, sharp gray-white differentiation, normal baseline flow voids.
Severity: Normal
