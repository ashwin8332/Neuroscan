        How a finding is produced

        1. The slice is cropped to the head and padded to a square, exactly as during training.
        2. Each trained model scores the slice and its mirror image. Over-confident models are calibrated on validation data so their scores are not inflated; no model is ever made more confident than it is.
        3. Models are combined with weights based on their validation accuracy. Missing models are left out, never guessed.
        4. If the top probability is below 60%, the top two classes are close, or the models disagree, the result is shown as inconclusive.

        Limitations

        • Only four categories are known. "No tumor pattern" does not exclude stroke, MS, bleeding or other conditions.
        • One 2-D slice is analyzed; radiologists review full 3-D studies with several sequences.
        • The public training data mixes sources and contains duplicates, so test scores are likely optimistic for new hospitals and scanners.
        • Grad-CAM heatmaps are coarse and are not tumor segmentations.
